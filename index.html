How this works
Each partner puts in their own money
Clenny and Clanny each put in a share of the product cost. Set both amounts under Shipment Setup.
The green check appears when both shares together cover the product cost.
Each partner logs their own operating costs
Clanny enters Clanny's costs. Clenny enters Clenny's costs. Nobody enters costs for the other partner.
Gas, rental, freight, pickup, mailing — anything paid out of pocket to move product.
Only approved business costs count
Set “Included in Settlement” to Yes for real business costs, No for anything personal or not-yet-paid.
Personal receipts and IOUs stay out of the payout math until they’re real business expenses.
Costs don’t split 50/50 automatically
Whoever paid for something gets credited for it. Their share of the eventual payout goes up accordingly.
Bigger contribution → bigger share of the profit.
Personal withdrawals come out of your payout
Taking cash from the pot for personal use? Log it in Ops as a Personal Withdrawal. Cash On Hand drops by that amount, and the same amount is deducted from the withdrawing partner's payout.
A withdrawal is an early payout — it never counts as a contribution and never raises your share.
Product taken personally is a sale
If either partner takes cans for themselves, record it under Sales — not Ops.
Keeps product taken out of operating costs where it doesn’t belong.
Cash collection is tracked separately from payout
Every sale records who physically collected the cash. Settlement shows what each partner is owed versus what they’re already holding.
One person can collect most of the cash without changing the payout — it just changes who owes whom at the end.
Target price is a planning number
Set the target sale price per can under Shipment Setup. It drives projected revenue and projected profit.
Update it any time the actual selling price changes.
